## Deliberate omissions and reconciliations

| Material | Decision |
| --- | --- |
| Bibliographies, study summaries, citations, dates of consultation | Omit from operational references; retain only task-relevant conclusions |
| Asset-by-asset counts, durations, and source-tour narrative | Retain reusable layout/motion lessons; omit the inventory |
| Conflicting candidate margins and timing ranges | Supply workable alternatives and labeled starting values, not universal requirements |
| Font capacity estimates versus actual measurements | Keep estimates for planning; require real string metrics for acceptance |
| Research preference for shorter copy versus product requirement | Prefer familiar complete wording and stable identity; use microcopy only when already conventional |
| Claims of gamma calibration from another controller | Explain the existing firmware transform; do not copy an unrelated LUT |
| Universal three-flash safety rule | Avoid full-field/critical flashing; no numeric safety certification from FPS |
| Brightness percentages, nits discrepancy, PWM/thermal constants | Do not invent values; physical checks remain explicit |
| Angular-size equations, study effect sizes, sample sizes, 90/95% targets | Omit from daily production instructions; do not turn experimental suggestions into mandatory acceptance |
| Extensive human-subject and instrumentation protocols | Keep bounded physical review conditions; no automatic study or deployment requirement |
| Generic state-machine schemas or schedulers | Explain state responsibilities without adding a parallel architecture |
| Twelve separate handoff manifests | Combine necessary information in the Markdown specification and existing JSON/QA artifacts |
| Research provenance versus asset licensing | Remove research provenance; retain operational source inputs and required asset notices |

The two reports do not have identical numeric recommendations. The revision selects usable starting examples and states their limits. It does not claim that all exploratory ranges are simultaneously mandatory or physically validated.
